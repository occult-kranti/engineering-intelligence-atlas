# Art references and Blender-to-game workflow

Use the dated [Daftar production plan](https://github.com/occult-kranti/engineering-intelligence-atlas/blob/main/projects/game-development-research/README.md)
and its two source audits to select task-specific references. Read
[Art fundamentals](https://github.com/occult-kranti/engineering-intelligence-atlas/blob/main/projects/game-development-research/ART_FUNDAMENTALS.md)
for drawing and modeling; read
[Pipeline and rendering](https://github.com/occult-kranti/engineering-intelligence-atlas/blob/main/projects/game-development-research/PIPELINE_AND_RENDERING.md)
for engine interoperability, rigging and compositing. These are research guides,
not evidence that an add-on is installed or a lesson was completed.

## Select a reference by output

- Perspective, gesture and anatomy: use Practice Drawing This as an interactive
  reference; produce original studies. Do not scrape or redistribute its models.
- Original game props: use the verified Grant Abbitt or CG Cookie lesson that
  addresses the required topology, UV, bake or material problem. Preserve the
  actual creator attribution and access conditions.
- Procedural geometry and lighting: select an actual Polyfjord, Ryan King Art or
  Ducky 3D lesson from the source audit. Do not assume the supplied hypothetical
  repository or course title exists.
- Prototype materials and environments: inspect the exact Poly Haven or Kenney
  item and retain its license/source record. Free access is not a universal asset
  license; keep third-party logos or other rights separate where applicable.
- Optional modeling tools: verify MACHIN3tools' current distribution, features,
  price and Blender compatibility. Native Blender is the default zero-purchase
  route. Do not claim unmeasured productivity multipliers.
- Rendering and VFX: use the verified Robin Squares, stache and InLightVFX
  references for color management, restrained rendering or compositing. A film
  result does not satisfy a gameplay capture requirement.

## Deliver a complete asset slice

1. Record the gameplay use, target engine/version, target device, visual purpose,
   units, source rights and measurable budgets.
2. Make original reference studies and an editable Blender source. Keep acquired
   masters immutable; store modeled, retopologized and exported derivatives under
   separate names and hashes.
3. Choose the format from the receiving engine's supported workflow. Unity's
   standard model route and Unreal's skeletal pipeline commonly use FBX; Unity
   glTF uses an explicit importer/package. Godot's documented glTF route differs
   from its FBX importer. Verify versions and asset-specific support from the
   linked official sources rather than using a universal format rule.
4. Probe with a small prop or rig first. Check scale, axes, hierarchy, normals,
   UVs, material maps, animation/skin deformation and root-motion policy. Decode
   unsupported compression extensions into a separate derivative if required.
5. Integrate the real mechanic. Record collision, LOD and animation behavior under
   input, not only a turntable render.
6. Profile the defined scene and device; retain frame-time percentiles and memory
   evidence. A 60 FPS goal is 16.67 ms per frame, not an observed result until tested.
7. Return source/export hashes, versions/settings, source and license ledger,
   actual results, unavailable checks and a concise next task.

Use existing OpenCodeGameStudios art/programming/engine/QA resources selectively.
Their role definitions do not grant permissions or create executing agents. MB-Lab
and legacy Blender add-on mirrors need explicit archive/compatibility review;
their historical existence does not establish current support.

For captured monuments, additionally read the [accuracy contract](https://github.com/occult-kranti/engineering-intelligence-atlas/blob/main/projects/architecture-library/site-models/ACCURACY.md).
Do not turn unknown source scale, preview import losses, missing interiors or
coarse relief into claims of surveyed or stone-level accuracy.
