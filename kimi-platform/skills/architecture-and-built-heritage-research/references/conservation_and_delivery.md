## Heritage gate

When historic fabric, archaeological material, or culturally significant context is involved:

1. Document significance, values, condition, provenance, ownership/custody, stakeholders, and prior interventions.
2. Use the minimum necessary intervention; preserve evidence and reversibility where feasible; distinguish additions/new work from historic fabric.
3. Do not reconstruct missing features conjecturally and present them as documented fact. Mark hypotheses and visual reconstructions explicitly.
4. Involve qualified conservators, archaeologists, architects, engineers, local communities/custodians, and relevant authorities.
5. Record all measurements, sources, translations, decisions, materials, and interventions. Use open/non-destructive documentation where appropriate.
6. Screen provenance and cultural-property concerns; do not advise removal, purchase, export, or alteration of artifacts without authorized professional/legal review.

Starting references: ICOMOS charters https://www.icomos.org/en/charters-and-texts and UNESCO culture conventions https://www.unesco.org/en/culture/conventions. Venice/Nara/Burra principles are applied through context and expert judgment, not as a generic aesthetic filter.

## Delivery and BIM evidence

Record project stage, purpose, audience, required information, units/CRS, model/authorship provenance, validation rules, clashes/issues, and handover owner. Use IFC for exchange where suitable, IDS for requirements, BCF for issues, and appropriate openBIM tooling. buildingSMART standards: https://www.buildingsmart.org/standards/; UK BIM Framework: https://ukbimframework.org/.

For 4D/5D work, validate model-element links to schedule/cost data, source versions, missing mappings, units/currency, temporal revisions, and responsible planner/estimator. Do not infer construction safety, cost certainty, or regulatory compliance from a linked model.

## Professional limitations

Agent output can support research, visualization, concept exploration, documentation, and deterministic checks. It is not a license to practice architecture or engineering and does not replace structural, fire, accessibility, planning, conservation, or construction review. Local codes and qualified professionals must approve real-world design and intervention. Clearly mark conceptual, historical, reconstructed, simulated, and unverified content.
