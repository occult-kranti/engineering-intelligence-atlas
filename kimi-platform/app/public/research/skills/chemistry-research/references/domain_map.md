# Chemistry Domain Map — 12 Disciplines

Derived from the chemistry research library (2026-11-20). Access notes: free/open unless marked otherwise; licenses and editions change — verify before citing or reusing content.

## 1. General Chemistry

Atomic structure, stoichiometry, thermochemistry, kinetics, equilibrium, acids/bases, electrochemistry. Canonical open textbooks: OpenStax *Chemistry 2e* and *Atoms First 2e* (CC BY 4.0, https://openstax.org/subjects/science); MIT OpenCourseWare 5.111/5.112 *Principles of Chemical Science* (CC BY-NC-SA, https://ocw.mit.edu); Chemistry LibreTexts (mixed CC licenses, check per-page, https://chem.libretexts.org).

## 2. Organic Chemistry

Structure and reactivity of carbon compounds; functional groups, mechanisms (nucleophilic substitution, elimination, addition, pericyclic, radical), stereochemistry, spectroscopy-driven structure elucidation, retrosynthetic logic. Nomenclature authority: IUPAC *Blue Book* (2013 recommendations, https://iupac.org/what-we-do/books/bluebook/). Open texts: LibreTexts Organic Chemistry; OpenStax partner content.

## 3. Inorganic Chemistry

Main-group, transition-metal, and organometallic chemistry; coordination compounds (ligand field theory, 18-electron rule), solid-state and bioinorganic chemistry. Nomenclature authority: IUPAC *Red Book* (2005, https://iupac.org/what-we-do/books/redbook/). Crystal structures: Crystallography Open Database (COD, https://www.crystallography.net/cod/, open) and ICSD (subscription; license boundary — do not bulk-redistribute).

## 4. Physical Chemistry

Thermodynamics, statistical mechanics, quantum mechanics, chemical kinetics, spectroscopy. Reference data: NIST Chemistry WebBook (thermochemistry, spectra; https://webbook.nist.gov/chemistry/) and NIST CCCBDB (https://cccbdb.nist.gov/). NIST data are US government works with a data-use disclaimer (https://www.nist.gov/oism/copyrights); some contributed data retain rights.

## 5. Analytical Chemistry

Qualitative and quantitative measurement: chromatography (GC/HPLC), mass spectrometry, atomic and molecular spectroscopy, electroanalytical methods, chemometrics, metrology in chemistry (traceability, calibration, method validation). Vocabulary per JCGM VIM (JCGM 200:2012, https://www.bipm.org/en/committees/jc/jcgm/publications) and the IUPAC *Orange Book* (Compendium of Analytical Nomenclature).

## 6. Theoretical Chemistry

Electronic structure theory (Hartree–Fock, post-HF correlation, density functional theory), basis sets, potential energy surfaces, reaction path theory. Terminology authority: IUPAC Gold Book (https://goldbook.iupac.org; cite per-entry DOI 10.1351/goldbook).

## 7. Computational Chemistry

Molecular mechanics, molecular dynamics, quantum chemistry, cheminformatics, machine-learning potentials. Open-source engines and reporting minimums in [resources.md](resources.md). ML-potential predictions are not measurements — label model identity and applicability domain.

## 8. Biochemistry

Biomolecular structure/function (proteins, nucleic acids, lipids, carbohydrates), enzyme kinetics, metabolism, signaling. Structure data: RCSB Protein Data Bank (https://www.rcsb.org; archive files CC0 1.0); small-molecule ontology: ChEBI (https://www.ebi.ac.uk/chebi/); bioactivity: ChEMBL (https://www.ebi.ac.uk/chembl/).

## 9. Materials Chemistry

Synthesis-structure-property relationships in solids: ceramics, semiconductors, catalysts, battery and photovoltaic materials, MOFs/zeolites. Computed property data: Materials Project (https://materialsproject.org; free account + API key; **computed** data subject to their Terms of Use and citation requirements); experimental structures: COD (open), ICSD/CSD (licensed).

## 10. Polymer Chemistry

Chain-growth vs. step-growth synthesis (radical, ionic, coordination, ring-opening), molecular weight distributions (Mn, Mw, dispersity Đ), glass transitions, structure-processing-property relations. IUPAC polymer nomenclature ("Purple Book" brief guide; full recommendations via https://iupac.org).

## 11. Environmental Chemistry

Fate and transport in air, water, soil; atmospheric photochemistry; water treatment; persistent pollutants (e.g., PFAS) as regulatory-science topics; green chemistry principles (Anastas & Warner's 12 principles, summarized by ACS, https://www.acs.org/greenchemistry/principles/12-principles-of-green-chemistry.html). GHS environmental hazard classes (acute/chronic aquatic toxicity; Rev 11 adds a global-warming hazard class — see [safety.md](safety.md)).

## 12. Medicinal Chemistry

Design and optimization of bioactive molecules: structure-activity relationships (SAR), ADME/Tox properties, Lipinski-type heuristics (guidelines, not laws), ligand efficiency, target validation. Data: ChEMBL (bioactivities), PubChem (https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov), PDB ligand structures. Discuss medicinal chemistry at the literature/informatics level only — never dosing or acquisition guidance (see [safety.md](safety.md)).
