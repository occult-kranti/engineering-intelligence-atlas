## Fibonacci, Liber Abaci, and Indian priority

**Verdict: the "Fibonacci sequence" recurrence has documented Indian priority; Fibonacci's historical role is transmitting Hindu–Arabic arithmetic to Europe, not discovering the sequence or its golden-ratio connection.**

Primary and scholarly record:

- Leonardo Pisano ("Fibonacci") wrote *Liber Abaci* in **1202**, revised **1228**; the 1202 manuscript does not survive. The name "Fibonacci" (filius Bonacci) was popularized by Guillaume Libri in 1838.
- The book's central project was introducing the **Hindu–Arabic numeral system** — which Leonardo called the *modus Indorum* ("method of the Indians") — to European commerce: place value, the nine figures plus 0, arithmetic algorithms, fractions, currency conversion, interest, barter, alloying. The title means "Book of Calculation" (computation *without* an abacus); practitioners were "algorists," from al-Khwārizmī, whence "algorithm."
- The rabbit problem generating the famous sequence occupies only a few pages of a 600+-page book. Leonardo computed to the 13th place (233) and did **not** connect the sequence to the golden ratio.
- The book synthesizes Hindu influences (Brahmagupta, Bhāskara II) and Islamic influences (al-Khwārizmī and successors) with Euclidean-style rigor.
- Editions and translations: first printed Latin edition, Boncompagni, Rome 1857; first English translation, Laurence Sigler, Springer 2002 (DOI 10.1007/978-1-4613-0079-3). Sigler also translated *Liber Quadratorum* (Academic Press, 1987); *Practica Geometriae* was translated by Barnabas Hughes (Springer, 2008).

Indian priority (well supported — always credit):

- The same recurrence arises in Sanskrit prosody (counting meters of short/long syllables): **Piṅgala**'s *Chandaḥśāstra* (variously dated ~450–200 BCE) contains the idea (*mātrāmeru*); **Virahāṅka** (~600–700 CE) stated the rule explicitly; **Gopāla** (~1135) quoted Virahāṅka; **Hemachandra** (~1150) presented it decades before Fibonacci.
- Key scholarship: Parmanand Singh, "The so-called Fibonacci Numbers in Ancient and Medieval India," *Historia Mathematica* 12 (1985) 229–244, DOI 10.1016/0315-0860(85)90021-7; Kim Plofker, *Mathematics in India* (Princeton UP, 2009).
- Édouard Lucas *named* the "Fibonacci sequence" in the 1870s; the Indian priority was recovered by 20th-century scholarship.
- Balanced popular account: Keith Devlin, *The Man of Numbers: Fibonacci's Arithmetic Revolution* (Walker, 2012).

## Golden-ratio folklore

**Verdict: aesthetic and monument claims about φ are 19th–20th-century inventions; do not repeat them as fact.**

- George Markowsky, "Misconceptions about the Golden Ratio," *College Mathematics Journal* 23(1):2–19 (1992), DOI 10.2307/2686193, debunks claims about the Great Pyramid, the Parthenon (whose obvious length/width ratio is 2.25 = 9/4, not φ≈1.618), Leonardo da Vinci's usage, and the aesthetic superiority of golden rectangles; the very name "golden ratio" is a 19th-century creation.
- Keith Devlin: the aesthetics claim traces primarily to Pacioli (misquoted) and Zeising (invention) — MAA columns "Good stories, pity they're not true" (2004) and "The Myth That Will Not Go Away" (2007).
- Clement Falbo measured nautilus shells and found mean ratios ≈1.33, not φ ("The Golden Ratio — A Contrary Viewpoint," *CMJ* 36(2):123–134, 2005, DOI 10.1080/07468342.2005.11922119).
- Mario Livio, *The Golden Ratio: The Story of Phi* (Broadway, 2002) separates the genuine mathematics of φ from the folklore.
- What *is* true: Kepler observed (1611) that ratios of consecutive Fibonacci numbers converge to φ; φ appears legitimately in Euclid's *Elements* Book XIII ("extreme and mean ratio") in the construction of regular solids; Binet's closed form F_n = ((1+√5)^n − (1−√5)^n)/(2^n√5).

## Newton audits

**Control statement:** use manuscripts, dated letters, edition-specific printed texts, and scholarly transcriptions before biographical narrative. Minimum archival route: [Cambridge University Digital Library: Newton Papers](https://cudl.lib.cam.ac.uk/collections/newton) → [Oxford Newton Project](https://www.newtonproject.ox.ac.uk/) → [SEP, "Isaac Newton"](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/newton/) → Royal Society records. Identify edition, date, language, and whether a text reflects published work or a manuscript draft. Key objects: Trinity College notebook [MS Add. 3996](https://cudl.lib.cam.ac.uk/view/MS-ADD-03996/1); Waste Book [MS Add. 4004](https://cudl.lib.cam.ac.uk/view/MS-ADD-04004/1); light-and-colours letter 1671/72, DOI [10.1098/rstl.1671.0072](https://doi.org/10.1098/rstl.1671.0072); *Principia* 1687/1713/1726 ([LOC 1687 copy](https://www.loc.gov/item/2021667054/)); *Opticks* 1704/1717-18; General Scholium (1713) transcription at the [Oxford Newton Project](https://www.newtonproject.ox.ac.uk/view/texts/normalized/NATP00551); 1675/76 Hooke letter ("shoulders of giants") at the [Historical Society of Pennsylvania](https://discover.hsp.org/Record/dc-9792/).

| Popular claim | Verdict | Controlling evidence |
|---|---|---|
| "An apple hit Newton and instantly produced universal gravitation." | **False as written.** Stukeley's 1752 memoir records Newton reflecting on why an apple falls perpendicularly; no head strike, no instant theory. | Royal Society's [Stukeley apple manuscript account](https://royalsociety.org/blog/2012/02/newtons-apple-tree/). |
| "Newton completed all his great discoveries in one plague year." | **Overcompressed.** The mid-1660s were extraordinarily productive, but the work matured over decades of manuscripts, correspondence, and revision. | Oxford [Newton Project introduction](https://www.newtonproject.ox.ac.uk/texts/introduction). |
| "Newton single-handedly invented calculus." | **Incomplete.** Newton developed fluxions early; Leibniz independently developed his calculus and published first; methods, notation, and transmission differ. Separate private invention, scribal circulation, print publication, notation, and influence. | [SEP Newton](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/newton/); *De analysi* context at [Oxford](https://www.newtonproject.ox.ac.uk/texts/introduction). |
| "Newton discovered gravity." | **Misleading.** Falling bodies, planetary laws, and inverse-square reasoning had prior and contemporary contributors; Newton's distinctive achievement was the mathematical universal synthesis. | *Principia* editions; [SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/newton/). |
| "*Hypotheses non fingo* means Newton rejected all hypotheses." | **False.** In the General Scholium he declined to feign an unsupported cause of gravity, while the *Opticks* Queries contain explicitly tentative speculation. | [General Scholium transcription](https://www.newtonproject.ox.ac.uk/view/texts/normalized/NATP00551); [Opticks guide](https://www.newtonproject.ox.ac.uk/tour/newtons-optical-work). |
| "Newton was simply a corpuscular theorist opposed to waves." | **Oversimplified.** His optics combined rays, periodic "fits," and medium/vibration language; do not map his terms onto modern photons. | 1718 *Opticks* Query text at [Oxford](https://www.newtonproject.ox.ac.uk/view/texts/normalized/NATP00051). |
| "Standing on the shoulders of giants" was Newton's original motto of humility. | **Partly false.** The sentence is in the 1675/76 letter to Hooke, but the metaphor has medieval antecedents; tone (courtesy vs. irony) is not settled by the sentence alone. | [HSP letter record](https://discover.hsp.org/Record/dc-9792/). |
| "The *Principia* is written in modern calculus notation." | **False.** The published presentation is predominantly geometrical and limit-based, though Newton possessed fluxional methods. | Annotated first edition, [Cambridge Adv.b.39.1](https://cudl.lib.cam.ac.uk/view/PR-ADV-B-00039-00001). |
| "Newton's theology and alchemy were irrelevant side hobbies." | **Historically untenable.** The manuscript corpus is extensive and intertwined with his conception of nature; interpret within its period. | [Oxford Newton Project](https://www.newtonproject.ox.ac.uk/) catalogue. |
| "Newton worked as an isolated lone genius." | **Overstated.** Barrow, Collins, Halley, Cotes, Hooke, Oldenburg, Flamsteed, and Royal Society institutions shaped communication, priority, data, and publication. | Oxford Newton Project; Royal Society records. |

**Newton research protocol:** report calendar convention for English dates between 1 January and 24 March; cite classmark and folio for manuscripts, edition and page for print; distinguish Newton's words, editorial modernization, translation, and later biography; for optical theory separate experimental observation, phenomenological law, and speculative mechanism; for *Principia* compare 1687/1713/1726 changes rather than citing a generic translation. Scholarly controls: [SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/newton/); Westfall, *Never at Rest*; Cohen's editions; Whiteside's *Mathematical Papers*; Shapiro's *Optical Papers*; Iliffe, *Priest of Nature*; Dobbs on alchemy — all interpretation, traced back to primary records.

## Tesla audits

**Control statement:** Tesla research is unusually vulnerable to hero myth, retrospective crediting, quotation distortion, and conspiracy framing. Controlling order: patent text and dated correspondence → contemporary lectures and publications → laboratory notebooks → archive finding aids → court records → critical biography and technical history. Never use a patent as proof of a successful commercial demonstration, an autobiography as proof of disputed priority, or an FBI file as proof that a spectacular technology existed. Principal scholarly control: W. Bernard Carlson, *Tesla: Inventor of the Electrical Age* ([Princeton UP](https://press.princeton.edu/books/paperback/9780691165615/tesla)). Key archives: [Nikola Tesla Museum, Belgrade](https://tesla-museum.org/en/); [UNESCO Memory of the World: Tesla Archive](https://memory.unesco.org/heritage/tesla-archive) (registered 2003 — documentary significance, not correctness of claims); [LOC Tesla correspondence 1890–1934](https://hdl.loc.gov/loc.mss/eadmss.ms014047); [Google Patents](https://patents.google.com/); [FBI Vault: Tesla](https://vault.fbi.gov/nikola-tesla); Martin's 1894 [*Inventions, Researches and Writings of Nikola Tesla*](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/39272/39272-h/39272-h.htm); *Colorado Springs Notes, 1899–1900*; [*My Inventions* (1919)](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/My_Inventions) as testimony, not proof.

Key patents (read independent claims first; record filing, priority/divisional history, issue date):

| Patent | Filing / issue | Establishes | Does not establish |
|---|---|---|---|
| [US381968A](https://patents.google.com/patent/US381968A/en), Electro-Magnetic Motor | Filed 1887-10-12; issued 1888-05-01 | Dated legal disclosure for the polyphase motor work | That Tesla invented all AC machinery or that modern induction motors descend unchanged from it |
| [US613809A](https://patents.google.com/patent/US613809A/en), remote control of vessels | Filed 1898-07-01; issued 1898-11-08 | Dated claims for wireless remote control | Autonomous robotics, military deployment, or modern digital remote control |
| [US645576A](https://patents.google.com/patent/US645576A/en), System of Transmission of Electrical Energy | Filed 1897-09-02; issued 1900-03-20 | Disclosed tuned transmission through natural media | Lossless global wireless power or energy creation |
| [US649621A](https://patents.google.com/patent/US649621A/en), Apparatus for Transmission of Electrical Energy | Divisional filed 1900-02-19; issued 1900-05-15 | Apparatus claims for the transmission system | A demonstration certificate, measured efficiency, or an operational network |

**Marconi v. United States, 320 U.S. 1 (1943) — controlled reading.** The [Supreme Court](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/320/1/) held broad claims of Marconi's later four-circuit tuning patent No. 763,772 invalid as anticipated, discussing Tesla, Lodge, and Stone. The opinion says Tesla anticipated a four-circuit tuned combination but lacked Lodge's variable-inductance adjustment; it relies principally on Stone for the invalidity analysis and expressly declines to decide whether Stone's patent itself involved invention; Marconi's first-transmission reputation rested on his original patent/reissue, not the claims under review. **Supported:** Tesla had legally significant prior disclosure relevant to tuned wireless circuits; Marconi's broad later tuning claims were invalid as anticipated in that litigation. **Not supported:** "The Supreme Court ruled Tesla was the sole inventor of radio."

| Popular claim | Verdict | Evidence controls |
|---|---|---|
| "Tesla invented alternating current." | **False.** AC generation and transformation predated Tesla; his contribution was a practical polyphase motor/transmission system in a crowded field (Ferraris, Dolivo-Dobrowolsky, Westinghouse engineers). | [US381968A](https://patents.google.com/patent/US381968A/en); 1888 AIEE lecture in [Martin 1894](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/39272/39272-h/39272-h.htm); Carlson. |
| "The War of the Currents was a two-person Edison–Tesla duel." | **Oversimplified.** Edison, Westinghouse, Elihu Thomson, utilities, financiers, standards, safety campaigns, and system economics all mattered. | Carlson; [ETHW Tesla biography](https://ethw.org/Nikola_Tesla). |
| "Tesla was the sole inventor of radio." | **False.** Wireless development involved Hertz, Lodge, Marconi, Fessenden, Stone, Tesla, and others; Tesla's tuned-circuit disclosures were important prior art, not a one-person history. | [320 U.S. 1](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/320/1/); [US645576A](https://patents.google.com/patent/US645576A/en), [US649621A](https://patents.google.com/patent/US649621A/en). |
| "Marconi's 1943 patent defeat proves Tesla won priority for all radio." | **False.** The Court invalidated particular broad claims of one later patent; it did not adjudicate the original Marconi patent or award a universal title. | Syllabus and opinion at [320 U.S. 1](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/320/1/). |
| "Wardenclyffe was a free-energy plant." | **False.** Patents and writings describe transmission of energy through natural media, not creation of energy; Wardenclyffe was unfinished and never demonstrated as a global power system. | [US645576A](https://patents.google.com/patent/US645576A/en); [US649621A](https://patents.google.com/patent/US649621A/en). |
| "Financiers destroyed Wardenclyffe because it would make electricity free." | **Unsupported as a single cause.** Funding, feasibility, competing systems, Tesla's business record, debt, and changing priorities all mattered; no archival proof of suppression because metering was impossible. | Tesla–Morgan correspondence via [LOC finding aid](https://hdl.loc.gov/loc.mss/eadmss.ms014047); Carlson. |
| "Tesla built and demonstrated a death ray." | **Unsubstantiated.** Tesla publicized "teleforce" claims; John G. Trump's review of the papers reported no new, sound, workable principles or methods for the claimed results. | [FBI Vault](https://vault.fbi.gov/nikola-tesla); Carlson. |
| "The FBI seized all Tesla papers and still hides a working weapon." | **Overstated.** The Office of Alien Property Custodian impounded effects; FBI records document interest, redactions, and uncertain handling, not a functioning device; the principal estate is in Belgrade. | [FBI Vault](https://vault.fbi.gov/nikola-tesla); [UNESCO Tesla Archive](https://memory.unesco.org/heritage/tesla-archive). |
| "The Colorado Springs photograph shows Tesla calmly beside live lightning." | **False.** Double exposure: discharge and seated Tesla were photographed separately; Tesla's own notebook note says the discharge was not playing when he was photographed. | *Colorado Springs Notes*; [Wikimedia Commons image provenance](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Tesla_colorado.jpg). |
| "Tesla lit 200 lamps wirelessly from 25 miles away in a controlled experiment." | **Unverified at that strength.** Accessible notes and contemporary evidence lack the instrumented chain (distance, lamp power, input power, efficiency, controls) needed to establish it. | *Colorado Springs Notes*; Carlson. |
| "Tesla refused a Nobel Prize because Edison would share it." | **Unsupported.** Tesla never received a Nobel Prize; nomination records and absence of an award do not prove refusal. | [Nobel nomination archive](https://www.nobelprize.org/nomination/redirector/?redir=archive%2F). |
| "Tesla predicted smartphones and the Internet exactly." | **Retrospective exaggeration.** He described wireless communication, portable receivers, news, and images, but not packet switching, TCP/IP, touchscreens, or semiconductor platforms. Quote exactly before interpreting. | [My Inventions](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/My_Inventions); 1904/1905 wireless writings. |
| "Tesla coils are safe toys because Tesla touched high-frequency apparatus." | **Dangerous myth.** High-voltage/high-frequency apparatus can cause burns, cardiac effects, fires, EMI, ozone/NOx, and hearing damage. Historical demonstrations are not operating instructions; route any physical replication to a qualified high-voltage laboratory. | Modern laboratory safety controls. |

**Tesla research protocol:** build the dated source matrix (private note → patent application → public lecture → demonstration → commercial installation → later recollection); cite claim number and figure for patents, never headline titles; for wireless claims state transmitter input power, receiver output, distance, antenna/ground geometry, frequency, coupling regime, losses, instrumentation, and uncertainty; distinguish near-field induction, radiative far field, conduction through Earth, and Tesla's "terrestrial stationary waves" language; use Carlson for context but follow its notes to primary material; compare O'Neill's 1944 biography cautiously; treat Tesla's recollections as evidence of what he remembered or wished to claim.
