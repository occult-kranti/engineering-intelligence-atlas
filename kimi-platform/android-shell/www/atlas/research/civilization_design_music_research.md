# PART I — Civilization art and design research

## 1. Standing rules against civilizational essentialism

1. **Geography + date + community, always.** Every artifact claim names the region, the date range, and the producing/patron community (dynasty, guild, workshop, lineage, religious order). "Egyptian style," "Indian art," or "Islamic geometry" as timeless unities are banned as load-bearing categories; they may appear only as historiographic labels with citation.
2. **Evidence tiers.** (a) surviving fabric/objects and measured survey; (b) contemporary texts, inscriptions, pattern scrolls, drawings; (c) documented craft practice from workshop evidence or recorded lineages; (d) later interpretation/revival; (e) contested or popular claims. Tiers (d)–(e) must be labeled and may never carry a verdict alone.
3. **No pseudohistory.** No hyper-diffusionism, lost-technology, or sacred-geometry claims; no retrojection of modern notation (e.g., wallpaper-group symbols) into makers' intentions; no golden-ratio or Fibonacci claims in art/architecture unless a primary design document shows it (see Markowsky 1992 audit below; the mathematics-research skill's safety note controls the recurrence/priority history).
4. **Proportion claims need documents.** "Proportion as documented practice" means: a treatise, drawing, canon grid, workshop manual, inscription, or consistent metrological study — not a measurement-fitted ratio on a photograph.
5. **Modern labs are labeled modern.** Every generative/SVG/CAD exercise below is a contemporary teaching artifact inspired by documented constructions; none is presented as a historical reconstruction or as "how they actually did it" unless a pattern scroll/manual exists.

## 2. Shared mathematical vocabulary (panel-wide)

- **Frieze (border) groups: 7.** **Wallpaper (plane crystallographic) groups: 17.** Classification: Fedorov (1891) and Pólya (1924) proofs; recognition procedure and notation in Doris Schattschneider, "The Plane Symmetry Groups: Their Recognition and Notation," *American Mathematical Monthly* 85 (1978): 439–450. Definitive reference: Branko Grünbaum & G. C. Shephard, *Tilings and Patterns* (W. H. Freeman, 1987; 2nd ed. Dover 2016).
- **Color symmetry.** Counting a colored pattern's group depends on whether color permutation is a symmetry; the Alhambra debate (§7.1) turned exactly on this. D. K. Washburn & D. W. Crowe, *Symmetries of Culture: Theory and Practice of Plane Pattern Analysis* (University of Washington Press, 1988) is the standard anthropological protocol; its follow-up *Symmetry Comes of Age* (2004) addresses field application.
- **Tilings.** Isohedral tilings (Kaplan's Tactile library, repo case), substitution/Penrose tilings (de Bruijn 1981 algebraic theory), and girih/polygonal strapwork systems (§7.2). Modern classification does not imply historical makers classified; it is an analytical lens — say so every time.
- **Transformations.** Rigid isometries (translation, rotation, reflection, glide reflection), similarity/dilation, and grid-based affine constructions are the working set for ornament analysis and for the SVG/CAD labs (§7).
- **Golden-ratio folklore control.** George Markowsky, "Misconceptions about the Golden Ratio," *College Mathematics Journal* 23 (1992): 2–19, https://doi.org/10.1080/07468342.1992.11973428 (verified 2026-11-21), plus Mario Livio, *The Golden Ratio* (2002). Claims about the Parthenon, pyramids, Nautilus, or "universal aesthetic phi" are folklore; the mathematics-research skill audit governs.

## 3. Roman world (regionally and period-anchored)

**Scope used here:** Italian peninsula and western provinces, late Republic through High Empire (c. 100 BCE–200 CE), for materials and documented design practice.

- **Textual layer.** Vitruvius, *De architectura* (c. 30–20 BCE) — the only surviving Greco-Roman architectural treatise; use the Loeb (Granger) or Rowland translation. Books III–IV give documented proportional rules for temples; Book VII gives pigments and wall-painting practice (Egyptian blue manufacture at Pozzuoli is described at 7.11). Treat Vitruvius as one Augustan-era author, not "the Roman method."
- **Materials evidence.** *Opus caementicium*: Seymour, Maragh, Sabatini, Di Tommaso, Weaver & Masic, "Hot mixing: Mechanistic insights into the durability of ancient Roman concrete," *Science Advances* 9(1) (2023) eadd1602, https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.add1602 (verified 2026-11-21) — lime clasts as self-healing reservoirs; authors explicitly contrast with Vitruvius's slaked-lime account (ancient texts vs. material evidence is the lesson). Marine concrete: Jackson et al., "Phillipsite and Al-tobermorite mineral cements produced through low-temperature water-rock reactions in Roman marine concrete," *American Mineralogist* 102(7) (2017): 1435–1450, https://doi.org/10.2138/am-2017-5993CCBY (verified 2026-11-21). A December 2025 *Nature Communications* study of an unfinished Pompeii construction site (79 CE) supplied direct workshop confirmation of dry pre-mixed quicklime practice — cite it as confirmation that evidence routes can overturn text-based assumptions; it is young and should be tracked for replication.
- **Proportion as documented practice.** Mark Wilson Jones, *Principles of Roman Architecture* (Yale, 2000) — argues from survey that Roman designers worked in whole-number modular and √2-type relationships (ad quadratum-type figures) using Roman foot modules; this is a measured-survey argument, explicitly distinguished from golden-ratio claims. The Pantheon (Hadrianic, c. 118–125 CE, Rome): coffer geometry and oculus documented by survey — see Rabun Taylor, *Roman Builders: A Study in Architectural Process* (Cambridge, 2003), and Lise Hetland's debated "Hadrian's Pantheon" thesis (2007) as an example of a live scholarly dispute to present as such.
- **Ornament and color.** Pompeian wall-painting "styles" (Mau's 1882 four-style typology) are a modern classification — teach it as such. Pigment evidence: Roman Egyptian blue (synthetic CaCuSi4O10) characterization via Getty Conservation Institute pigment studies and Giovanni Verri's VIL (visible-induced luminescence) imaging work; Cennino Cennini's *Il Libro dell'Arte* (c. 1400) and Theophilus Presbyter, *De diversis artibus* (12th c.) are the later craft-text anchors for pigment recipes.
- **Failure modes to reject:** "Romans invented concrete and we lost the recipe forever" (the materials literature above is more precise and more interesting); bare assertion that the Pantheon encodes the golden ratio (Markowsky audit); treating Renaissance codification of the orders as unchanged Roman practice (already gated in the architecture skill).

## 4. Gothic (Latin Christian Europe, c. 1140–1500, with named sites)

- **Specific anchors:** Saint-Denis chevet under Abbot Suger (1140–44, Île-de-France); Chartres Cathedral rebuild (from 1194, diocese of Chartres; cobalt-rich glazing); Cologne Cathedral (begun 1248, Rhineland lodge tradition); Regensburg lodge (Roriczer).
- **Documented design practice — the strongest evidence tier:** (a) the portfolio of Villard de Honnecourt (c. 1225–35, Paris, BnF MS fr. 19093 — Picard lodge context; drawings are lodge teaching aids, not construction documents); (b) Mathes Roriczer, *Büchlein von der Fialen Gerechtigkeit* (Regensburg, 1486) — the earliest printed lodge manual, gives an explicit ad quadratum rotation-of-squares procedure for pinnacle elevation; (c) surviving full-scale parchment plans, e.g., Strasbourg Plan B (13th c.). Standard synthesis: Lonnie R. Shelby, *Gothic Design Techniques: The Fifteenth-Century Design Booklets of Mathes Roriczer and Hanns Schmuttermayer* (Southern Illinois University Press, 1977); Shelby, "The Geometrical Knowledge of Mediaeval Master Masons," *Speculum* 47 (1972): 395–421.
- **The ad quadratum / ad triangulum debate is the honesty model.** James S. Ackerman, "'Ars Sine Scientia Nihil Est': Gothic Theory of Architecture at the Cathedral of Milan," *Art Bulletin* 31 (1949): 84–111 — a documented 1390s dispute at Milan Cathedral between Lombard masters and northern consultants over square- vs. triangle-based elevation rules. Use it to show proportional systems were argued, plural, and recorded — never a single secret canon.
- **Modern structural analysis:** Robert Bork, *The Geometry of Creation: Architectural Drawing and the Dynamics of Gothic Design* (Ashgate, 2011) reconstructs draft sequences from measured plans; Robert Mark's photoelastic modeling (*Experiments in Gothic Structure*, MIT Press, 1982) tested structural hypotheses physically. Both are modern analyses — label them.
- **Color/material:** grisaille vs. full-color glazing economics; silver-stain (early 14th c.); medieval glass chemistry — Roy Newton & Sandra Davison, *Conservation of Glass* (1989). Erwin Panofsky's scholasticism analogy (*Gothic Architecture and Scholasticism*, 1951) is a famous but contested reading — present as interpretation.
- **Failure modes:** "Gothic cathedrals encode the golden ratio/π" (no lodge document shows this; Bork's work shows documented quadrature/triangulation instead); treating Gothic as pan-European uniformity (rayonnant vs. perpendicular vs. Sondergotik differ regionally).

## 5. Indian regions/periods (five anchored cases — deliberately not "Indian architecture")

1. **Chola Thanjavur: Brihadisvara (Rajarajesvara) temple, Thanjavur, Tamil Nadu — consecrated 1010 CE, patron Rajaraja I Chola.** The temple's own inscription corpus is a primary source for patronage, endowments, and personnel; monument: axial vimana in granite. Sources: Archaeological Survey of India, https://asi.nic.in/ (verified 2026-11-21); George Michell, *The Hindu Temple* (1977). Distinct from the arch-skill's Śulba/Vastu route: here the evidence is epigraphic and material.
2. **Hoysala Karnataka: Chennakesava temple, Belur (commissioned 1117 CE by Vishnuvardhana) and Hoysaleswara temple, Halebidu (c. 1120–1160; master mason Ketamalla named in inscription).** Stellate (star-plan) platform geometry, lathe-finished chloritic-schist pillars, horizontal frieze banding (elephants/lions/horses/epic scroll) — a strong frieze-group teaching case (most registers classify as translation-based pm-family patterns; classify with criteria, don't gush). Source: Adam Hardy, *Indian Temple Architecture: Form and Transformation* (1995) — the aedicule-accretion model of Karnata-Dravida composition; ASI records.
3. **Early-medieval Rajasthan: Chand Baori stepwell, Abhaneri, Dausa district (c. 8th–9th c. CE, Nikumbha/Chahamana-period context; dating debated — say so).** ~3,500 steps in double-flight zigzag geometry; ideal modern case for transformation-geometry labs requiring no mystical claims. Sources: Morna Livingston, *Steps to Water: The Ancient Stepwells of India* (Princeton Architectural Press, 2002); ASI.
4. **Chandela Khajuraho, Madhya Pradesh: Western Group, Kandariya Mahadeva temple (c. 1025–1050, patron Vidyadhara).** Nagara superstructure geometry in sandstone; the erotic panels are a minority of the sculptural program — reject the popular framing that reduces the site to them. Source: Devangana Desai, *The Religious Imagery of Khajuraho* (1996); ASI.
5. **Mughal Agra/Delhi (1526–1707, Timurid-descended court):** Humayun's Tomb, Delhi (c. 1569–1572; architect Mirak Mirza Ghiyas, a Persian émigré; hasht bihisht ninefold plan; red sandstone/white marble); Tomb of I'timad-ud-Daulah, Agra (1622–1628; patron Nur Jahan; the pietra-dura transition case); Taj Mahal, Agra (1632–1653; Ustad Ahmad Lahori; charbagh; pietra dura with documented stone types). Source: Ebba Koch, *Mughal Architecture* (1991) and *The Complete Taj Mahal* (2006). Anti-claim: no primary evidence supports "Taj golden ratio" assertions; documented practice is grid-and-module planning per Koch's plan analyses.
- **Textual layer with dating honesty:** Mānasāra and Mayamata śilpaśāstras (dating contested; treat as compiled practice texts, not a single timeless "vastu science"); Stella Kramrisch, *The Hindu Temple* (1946) — classic but interpretive; Michael W. Meister's morphological essays for philological method.

## 6. Dynastic Egypt (c. 2686–332 BCE; ornament, color, canon — construction routes stay in the arch skill)

- **The proportion canon is the cleanest documented-practice case in world art.** Gay Robins, *Proportion and Style in Ancient Egyptian Art* (University of Texas Press, 1994): artists used a square grid — 18 squares hairline-to-sole for standing figures (Old/Middle Kingdom), 21 squares in the Third Intermediate/Late periods; the Amarna period (Akhenaten, c. 1353–1336 BCE) shows documented grid-based change with elongated forms. Grids survive on unfinished reliefs — primary evidence, not inference.
- **Materials/color:** Egyptian blue (synthetic calcium copper tetrasilicate, the first documented synthetic pigment, from the 4th Dynasty); faience (glazed non-clay ceramic). Chemistry: Heinz Berke, "The Invention of Blue and Purple Pigments in Ancient Times," *Chemical Society Reviews* 36 (2007): 15–30, https://doi.org/10.1039/B606268G (verified 2026-11-21). Rutherford Gettens & George Stout, *Painting Materials: A Short Encyclopaedia* (1942; Dover) for identification data.
- **Specific anchors:** Giza pyramid complexes (4th Dynasty, c. 2580–2500 BCE — Digital Giza, https://giza.fas.harvard.edu/, already the arch skill's evidence hub; reuse it, don't duplicate); Deir el-Bahari, Hatshepsut's mortuary temple (18th Dynasty, c. 1470 BCE); Amarna (Sculptor Thutmose's studio finds, 1912 Borchardt excavation — provenance history matters); Tutankhamun (KV62, 1922 Carter) as material-culture density case.
- **Math-in-practice:** Corinna Rossi, *Architecture and Mathematics in Ancient Egypt* (Cambridge, 2004) — documented practice used simple whole-number slopes (seked) and modular planning, against esoteric readings. Pyramid fringe theories remain gated by the arch skill; Robins/Rossi give the proportion discipline.
- **Calligraphy:** hieroglyphic signs are a calligraphic system with registers and orientation conventions; grammar: James P. Allen, *Middle Egyptian* (3rd ed., Cambridge, 2014). "Hieroglyphs as magical signs" readings are later (Horapollo) or modern-mystical layers.


## 7. Additional traditions (each anchored; expand by the same method, never by analogy)

### 7.1 Nasrid Alhambra, Granada (14th c.; patrons Yusuf I, Muhammad V) — the symmetry-group honesty case
- Edith Müller's 1944 Zürich dissertation (*Gruppentheoretische und strukturanalytische Untersuchungen der maurischen Ornamente aus der Alhambra in Granada*) classified 11 of the 17 groups. Grünbaum, Grünbaum & Shephard, "Symmetry in Moorish and Other Ornaments," *Computers & Mathematics with Applications* 12B (1986): 641–653, found at most 13. Pérez-Gómez (1987) claimed the missing four. Branko Grünbaum, "What Symmetry Groups Are Present in the Alhambra?", *Notices of the AMS* 53(6) (2006): 670–673, https://www.ams.org/notices/200606/comm-grunbaum.pdf (verified 2026-11-21), challenged loose classification and color-symmetry handling. B. Lynn Bodner, "The Planar Crystallographic Groups Represented at the Alhambra," *Bridges 2013 Proceedings*: 225–232, https://archive.bridgesmathart.org/2013/bridges2013-225.pdf (verified 2026-11-21), reports 14 under explicit criteria.
- **Panel rule:** the popular claim "the Alhambra contains all 17 wallpaper groups" is contested, and the dispute itself (criteria, color treatment, single-tile vs. extended pattern) is the teaching content. Any classification output must state criteria and cite which census it follows.

### 7.2 Timurid/Turkmen pattern scrolls and girih geometry — Topkapı Scroll, Topkapı Palace Museum Library MS H.1956 (15th–16th c. compilations)
- Gülru Necipoğlu, *The Topkapı Scroll: Geometry and Ornament in Islamic Architecture* (Getty, 1995) — the scroll is primary evidence that designers transmitted girih (strapwork) and muqarnas patterns as annotated 2D projections.
- Lu & Steinhardt, "Decagonal and Quasi-Crystalline Tilings in Medieval Islamic Architecture," *Science* 315 (2007): 1106–1110, https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1135491 (verified 2026-11-21) — girih-tile reconstruction of Darb-i Imam (Isfahan, 1453) as near-quasicrystalline. **Mandatory counterweights:** Makovicky's comment and the authors' reply, *Science* 318 (2007): 1383, and Peter R. Cromwell, "Cognitive Bias and Claims of Quasiperiodicity in Traditional Islamic Patterns," *Mathematical Intelligencer* 37(4) (2015): 30–44 — the quasiperiodicity claim is disputed and must be presented with the dispute.
- Craig S. Kaplan & David H. Salesin, "Islamic Star Patterns in Absolute Geometry," *ACM Transactions on Graphics* 23(2) (2004): 97–119, https://doi.org/10.1145/990002.990003 (verified 2026-11-21) — the modern polygons-in-contact (PIC) construction used in lab L2; modern synthesis inspired by Hankin (1925) and the scroll record, credited as such.

### 7.3 Arabic calligraphy as documented proportion — Abbasid Iraq and Ifriqiya
- Ibn Muqla (d. 940 CE, Baghdad, Abbasid vizier-calligrapher) is credited in the tradition with al-khatt al-mansub ("proportioned script"): letterforms measured in rhombic dots generated by the nib, with alif and circle as module — documented via later manuals; see Yasser Tabbaa, "The Transformation of Arabic Writing: Part I, Qur'anic Calligraphy," *Ars Orientalis* 21 (1991): 119–148. Specific object anchor: the "Blue Quran" (9th–10th c., gold Kufic on indigo-dyed vellum, Aghlabid/Fatimid Ifriqiyan milieu, likely Qayrawan; dispersed folios — provenance caution). Source: Jonathan Bloom, "The Blue Koran: An Early Fatimid Kufic Manuscript from the Maghrib," in *Les manuscrits du Moyen-Orient* (1989).
- Rule: calligraphy analysis names script style (Kufic variants, naskh, thuluth…), region, date, and support; numerological "sacred geometry of letters" content is out.

### 7.4 Classic Maya, Palenque (Chiapas, Mexico; 7th c. CE, reign of K'inich Janaab' Pakal, 615–683)
- Temple of the Inscriptions (completed c. 683) with Pakal's sarcophagus lid; glyphic calligraphy + modeled stucco + painted relief. Sources: Simon Martin & Nikolai Grube, *Chronicle of the Maya Kings and Queens* (2nd ed., 2008); Linda Schele & Mary Ellen Miller, *The Blood of Kings* (1986); Mesoweb (https://www.mesoweb.com/) and FAMSI (http://www.famsi.org/) primary-image archives (verified 2026-11-21). The Dresden Codex is the surviving folded-screen manuscript anchor for calendrics — no "Maya predicted 2012" content, ever.

### 7.5 Asante adinkra and Djenné adobe — Ghana and Mali, community-named
- Adinkra cloth stamping: Asante peoples, Ghana; Ntonso village (Kumasi region) as the documented production community; carved calabash stamps, bark dye; named motifs with documented meanings (Gye Nyame, Sankofa). Source: R. S. Rattray, *Religion and Art in Ashanti* (Oxford, 1927 — colonial-era; pair with modern Akan-symbolism scholarship and British Museum/Met collection records). Production is living and commercial; no "ancient secret symbols" framing.
- Great Mosque of Djenné, Mali: Sudano-Sahelian adobe; the current building dates to 1907 (colonial-era reconstruction on the earlier site — say this; "13th-century mosque" is wrong for the standing structure); the annual *crépissage* community replastering is the living-practice anchor. Source: Trevor Marchand, *The Masons of Djenné* (2009); UNESCO World Heritage listing (1988, Old Towns of Djenné ensemble).

### 7.6 Inca Cusco (15th c., reign of Pachacuti and successors)
- Ashlar masonry of Sacsayhuamán and Cusco walls: polygonal coursed stonework documented by replication experiment. Source: Jean-Pierre Protzen, *Inca Architecture and Construction at Ollantaytambo* (Oxford, 1993) and his joint-fitting replication studies; Susan Niles, *The Shape of Inca History* (1999) on Cusco as royal design. Reject "impossible precision = lost technology" framing; Protzen shows documented feasible method.

# PART II — Music theory and audio production engineering

## 10. Standing rules (music panel)

1. **Theory is descriptive, not universal.** Western common-practice harmony is one documented tradition (with its own regional and period variation), not the physics of music. Culturally specific theory (raga, tala, maqam, gamelan, West African rhythm) is taught only in named regional/community context, from tradition-internal or peer-reviewed ethnomusicological sources, and never homogenized into "world music theory."
2. **Psychoacoustics ≠ aesthetics.** Perceptual findings (consonance, loudness, masking) constrain but do not legislate musical value.
3. **Tuning claims are numeric.** State ratios/cents with the reference pitch and the temperament; "pure" and "natural" are banned as unqualified terms.
4. **Every audio artifact has a provenance chain:** source recordings/samples with license state, synthesis parameters/seeds, processing chain with tool versions, evaluation protocol, and rights status of the output.
5. **Legal uncertainty is reported as legal uncertainty.** US sampling law is circuit-split (below); AI-training litigation is unresolved (below); nothing here is legal advice — route rights decisions to counsel via the creative-rights-reproducibility-editor gate.

## 11. Acoustics and psychoacoustics (foundations)

- **Core texts:** Neville Fletcher & Thomas Rossing, *The Physics of Musical Instruments* (2nd ed., Springer, 1998); Thomas Rossing, F. Richard Moore & Paul Wheeler, *The Science of Sound* (3rd ed., 2002). Perception: Brian C. J. Moore, *An Introduction to the Psychology of Hearing* (6th ed., Brill, 2012); Eberhard Zwicker & Hugo Fastl, *Psychoacoustics: Facts and Models* (2nd ed., Springer, 1999) — critical bands, masking, loudness models.
- **Standards:** ISO 226:2023 (equal-loudness-level contours — cite the 2023 revision, not the Fletcher–Munson curves as current standard), https://www.iso.org/standard/83117.html (verified 2026-11-21); ITU-R BS.1770-5 loudness algorithm and EBU R128 for program loudness (LUFS) in delivery, https://www.itu.int/rec/R-REC-BS.1770/ and https://tech.ebu.ch/docs/r/r128.pdf (verified 2026-11-21); AES standards via https://www.aes.org/publications/standards/ (verified 2026-11-21).
- **Key quantitative facts the panel uses:** harmonic series as integer frequency ratios; just-noticeable-difference values are context-dependent (state test conditions); room acoustics via Sabine/Eyring reverberation formulas with stated assumptions (ISO 3382 for measurement).

## 12. Tuning and temperament (numeric discipline)

- **Cents:** cents = 1200·log2(f2/f1). 12-TET semitone = 100 cents; just fifth 3:2 = 701.955 cents; syntonic comma 81:80 = 21.5 cents; Pythagorean comma = 23.46 cents. Any temperament description states which comma(s) it distributes and where.
- **History with priority accuracy:** Zhu Zaiyu (朱載堉), Ming prince, published equal-temperament calculations in *Lülü jingyi* (1584, preface; calculations documented earlier in the 1580s) — the earliest documented computation of equal temperament to high precision; Simon Stevin's manuscript (*Van de Spiegheling der Singconst*, c. 1585–1605, unpublished until 1884) is the independent European computation. Source: Fritz A. Kuttner, "Prince Chu Tsai-Yü's Life and Work: A Re-Evaluation of His Contribution to Equal Temperament Theory," *Ethnomusicology* 19(2) (1975): 163–206. Neither implies the other; no diffusionist claim.
- **Reference works:** J. Murray Barbour, *Tuning and Temperament: A Historical Survey* (1951; Dover 2004); William Sethares, *Tuning, Timbre, Spectrum, Scale* (2nd ed., Springer, 2005) — the timbre–consonance interaction that justifies non-12-tone systems on acoustic grounds; Harry Partch, *Genesis of a Music* (2nd ed., 1974) as a documented idiosyncratic just-intonation practice (his 43-tone system is his own instrument-bound practice, not "natural tuning").
- **Well temperament ≠ equal temperament:** Owen Jorgensen, *Tuning* (1991), and the Barnes/Lindley debate on Bach's WTC tunings (John Barnes, "Bach's Keyboard Temperament: Internal Evidence from the Well-Tempered Clavier," *Early Music* 7(2) (1979): 236–249) — any claim about "Bach's temperament" must be labeled hypothesis; there is no surviving Bach tuning instruction. Kellner's squiggle hypothesis is popular folklore-level — never cite as settled.
- **Modern tool lesson:** Scala .scl/.kbm format (Manuel Op de Coul's Scala program) is the de facto exchange format for microtonal tunings; `surge-synthesizer/tuning-library` (MIT — repo case) is its maintained open implementation.

## 13. Harmony, counterpoint, rhythm, form, timbre (Western tradition, dated)

- **Counterpoint:** Johann Joseph Fux, *Gradus ad Parnassum* (1725; Alfred Mann's English translation, *The Study of Counterpoint*, 1943/1965) — species method as documented 18th-century pedagogy. Knud Jeppesen, *Counterpoint: The Polyphonic Vocal Style of the Sixteenth Century* (1939 English ed.) for Palestrina-style analysis. Teach as historical styles, not universal rules.
- **Harmony:** Walter Piston, *Harmony* (5th ed., 1987); open-access alternative: *Open Music Theory* (2nd ed., 2022, Vanderbilt University Library), https://viva.pressbooks.pub/openmusictheory/ (verified 2026-11-21, CC BY-SA) — a genuinely open textbook with integrated workbook. Schenkerian analysis: Heinrich Schenker, *Der freie Satz* (1935; English *Free Composition*, Oster trans., 1979) — influential, contested, and ideologically loaded in origin; teach as one analytical school with its critics (e.g., critiques in *Journal of Music Theory* and musicology-of-the-canon literature).
- **Rhythm/meter:** Justin London, *Hearing in Time* (2nd ed., Oxford, 2012) — meter as entrainment, cross-culturally careful. Grosvenor Cooper & Leonard B. Meyer, *The Rhythmic Structure of Music* (1960) for grouping. Formal theory: William Caplin, *Classical Form* (Oxford, 1998); Lerdahl & Jackendoff, *A Generative Theory of Tonal Music* (MIT, 1983) — landmark but not empirically settled; label as theory.
- **Mathematical rhythm analysis (modern, credited):** Godfried T. Toussaint, "The Euclidean Algorithm Generates Traditional Musical Rhythms," *Bridges 2005*: 47–56, and *The Geometry of Musical Rhythm* (2nd ed., CRC Press, 2019) — Bjorklund's accelerator algorithm (2003) rediscovered as generating maximally-even patterns matching documented timelines (e.g., the Cuban tresillo E(3,8), the 12/8 bell pattern E(5,12) family — note Toussaint's attributions come from ethnomusicological literature and must not be flattened into "all cultures share one rhythm"; see §14.4). Demaine et al., "The Distance Geometry of Music," *Computational Geometry* 42(5) (2009): 429–454 for rhythm similarity measures.
- **Timbre:** John M. Grey, "Multidimensional Perceptual Scaling of Musical Timbres," *JASA* 61(5) (1977): 1270–1277 — the classic timbre-space study; Stephen McAdams & Bruno Giordano, "The Perception of Musical Timbre," in *The Oxford Handbook of Music Psychology* (2nd ed., 2016).

## 14. Culturally specific theory (anchored cases only)

### 14.1 Hindustani raga/tala (North India; not "Indian music")
- Tradition-internal sources: Vishnu Narayan Bhatkhande's early-20th-c. systematization (*Hindustani Sangeet Paddhati*, 1910–1932; thath-based classification) — a modern codification, not ancient doctrine; N. A. Jairazbhoy, *The Rāgs of North Indian Music: Their Structure and Evolution* (1971/1995). Historiographic caution: Janaki Bakhle, *Two Men and Music: Nationalism in the Making of an Indian Classical Tradition* (Oxford, 2005) documents the colonial-era/nationalist reframing of "classical" — required reading before any pan-Indian claim.
- Tala: Hindustani tala cycles (e.g., tintal 16-beat) with theka practice; distinguish from Carnatic suladi sapta tala system (7 talas × 5 jatis) — different systems, different regions.

### 14.2 Carnatic theory (South India)
- P. Sambamoorthy, *South Indian Music* (6 vols.); the 72-melakarta system codified by Venkatamakhin (*Chaturdandi Prakasika*, c. 17th c., Thanjavur court context). Raga as melodic grammar (arohana/avarohana plus characteristic phrases), not a scale; performance practice differs from text lists — say so.

### 14.3 Maqam (Arab/Ottoman/Persian spheres — do not merge)
- Historical theory: al-Farabi, *Kitab al-Musiqi al-Kabir* (10th c.); Safi al-Din al-Urmawi, *Kitab al-Adwar* (13th c., Baghdad) — the first systematic modal treatise with letter notation. Modern practice: Scott Marcus, *Music in Egypt: Experiencing Music, Expressing Culture* (Oxford, 2007) and Ali Jihad Racy, *Making Music in the Arab World: The Culture and Artistry of Tarab* (Cambridge, 2003) — maqam as jins (tetrachord/trichord) composition with regionally variable intonation; the 1932 Cairo Congress quarter-tone standardization was contested then and now — present as a modernization project, not the tradition.
- Intonation honesty: measured practice varies by performer/region; do not assert a single "maqam tuning."

### 14.4 Gamelan (Java and Bali are different — name which)
- Jaap Kunst, *Music in Java* (3rd ed., 1973); Sumarsam, *Gamelan: Cultural Interaction and Musical Development in Central Java* (Chicago, 1995); Michael Tenzer, *Balinese Music* (1991) and *Gamelan Gong Kebyar* (2000). **Critical fact:** slendro and pelog are interval *categories*, and each gamelan set has its own tuning (embuh) — there is no standard slendro tuning; colotomic structure (gong-cycle punctuation) is the documented organizing principle. Never state "gamelan uses equal-tempered pentatonic."

### 14.5 West African rhythm (named peoples/repertoires only)
- V. Kofi Agawu, *African Rhythm: A Northern Ewe Perspective* (Cambridge, 1995) — both the canonical Northern Ewe (Ghana) account and a critique of homogenizing "African rhythm" discourse; pair with David Locke, *Drum Gahu: An Introduction to African Rhythm* (1998) for a documented Ewe dance-drumming repertoire; Simha Arom, *African Polyphony and Polyrhythm* (Cambridge, 1991) for Central African (Aka) polyphony with transcription method; Gerhard Kubik, *Theory of African Music* (2 vols., 2010).
- **Rule:** "West African rhythm is polymetric" is a homogenizing summary; teach specific timelines (e.g., the gankogui bell pattern of Gahu) with repertoire and community named, and cite the critique (Agawu, "The Invention of 'African Rhythm'," *JAMS* 48(3) (1995): 380–395).

## 15. Notation, MIDI, and data formats

- **Common Western notation:** Kurt Stone, *Music Notation in the Twentieth Century* (1980); Elaine Gould, *Behind Bars* (Faber, 2011) — current engraving standard.
- **MusicXML 4.x:** W3C Music Notation Community Group, https://www.w3.org/2021/06/musicxml40/ and repo `w3c/musicxml` (repo case; no repo-level LICENSE — governed by W3C Community Contributor License Agreement; MusicXML 4.0 Recommendation published 2021). Originated by Michael Good (Recordare→MakeMusic), donated to the W3C CG in 2015.
- **MEI (Music Encoding Initiative):** TEI-style XML for scholarly encoding, https://music-encoding.org/ (verified 2026-11-21) — the scholarly-edition choice. **Humdrum `**kern`:** Craig Stuart Sapp's toolkit for computational analysis, https://www.humdrum.org/ (verified 2026-11-21). **SMuFL** (Standard Music Font Layout, SIL OFL font metadata standard), https://w3c.github.io/smufl/latest/ (verified 2026-11-21) — with the Bravura reference font.
- **MIDI:** MIDI 1.0 Detailed Specification (MIDI Manufacturers Association, 1996 ed., at https://midi.org/specs — verified 2026-11-21); MIDI 2.0 core spec + MIDI-CI + MPE (MPE is the MIDI 1.0-era per-note-expression convention adopted into the MIDI 2.0 ecosystem). Teaching rule: MIDI encodes performance events, not audio; note numbers are convention (middle C = 60 in the common numbering — state the convention).
- **Audio formats/codecs:** WAV/BWF (EBU Tech 3285), FLAC (lossless, RFC 9639), MP3/AAC/Opus with stated bitrates; Opus = RFC 6716 — royalty-free, the modern open choice.

## 16. DSP, synthesis, and DAW engineering

- **Free, rigorous DSP texts:** Julius O. Smith III (CCRMA, Stanford), *Introduction to Digital Filters with Audio Applications* and *Physical Audio Signal Processing*, free at https://ccrma.stanford.edu/~jos/ (verified 2026-11-21); his *Spectral Audio Signal Processing* (W3K, 2011) likewise free online.
- **Reference texts:** Udo Zölzer (ed.), *DAFX: Digital Audio Effects* (2nd ed., Wiley, 2011); Joshua Reiss & Andrew McPherson, *Audio Effects: Theory, Implementation and Application* (CRC, 2014); Will Pirkle, *Designing Audio Effect Plugins in C++* (2nd ed., 2019); Vesa Välimäki & colleagues' work on physical modeling and artificial reverberation.
- **Sampling discipline:** Nyquist–Shannon (cite Claude Shannon, "Communication in the Presence of Noise," *Proc. IRE* 37 (1949) — already a mathematics-research primary); aliasing, quantization (dither/noise shaping — Lipshitz, Wannamaker & Vanderkooy, "Quantization and Dither," *JAES* 40(5) 1992), and stated sample rates in every artifact.
- **Synthesis methods:** subtractive/FM (John Chowning, "The Synthesis of Complex Audio Spectra by Means of Frequency Modulation," *JAES* 21(7) 1973 — the DX7's basis, patent-licensed historically), additive, wavetable, granular (Curtis Roads, *Microsound*, MIT 2001), physical modeling (Karplus–Strong 1983; Smith's waveguides).
- **Plugin/API landscape with license posture:** Web Audio API (W3C Recommendation; AudioWorklet for sample-rate DSP — repo case `WebAudio/web-audio-api`, W3C Software and Document License); JUCE (dual-licensed: GPLv3/commercial, plus free tier with revenue cap — check current terms, do not assume); VST3 SDK (Steinberg proprietary license); LV2 (open, ISC-style); FAUST (`grame-cncm/faust`, compiler GPL — generated DSP code takes the library license; verify per-use); Pure Data (BSD-style), Csound (LGPL-2.1) and SuperCollider (GPL-3.0) as scriptable server/synthesis engines (repo cases); Tone.js (MIT) for browser scheduling (repo case).
- **DAW reality:** no open standard for full project interchange; AAF/OMF (AMWA) and the newer DAWproject format (Bitwig+PreSonus, open, https://github.com/bitwig/dawproject — not inspected this pass; verify before citing) exist for interchange; session reproducibility means freezing stems + recording plugin versions/settings, not assuming project portability.

## 17. Music information retrieval and reproducible evaluation

- **Textbook:** Meinard Müller, *Fundamentals of Music Processing* (2nd ed., Springer, 2021) — open access, https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-3-030-69808-9 (verified 2026-11-21), with the accompanying Python notebooks (FMP notebooks).
- **Libraries (repo cases):** librosa (ISC; McFee et al., "librosa: Audio and Music Signal Analysis in Python," *Proc. SciPy* 2015, https://doi.org/10.25080/Majora-7b98e3ed-003); Essentia (AGPL-3.0 — note the copyleft posture for product use; Bogdanov et al., "Essentia: An Audio Analysis Library for Music Information Retrieval," *ACM MM* 2013, https://doi.org/10.1145/2502081.2502229); mir_eval (MIT; Raffel et al., "mir_eval: A Transparent Implementation of Common MIR Metrics," ISMIR 2014) — the evaluation-metrics reference implementation.
- **Datasets with license posture:** MAESTRO (Hawthorne et al., ICLR 2019 — CC BY-NC-SA 4.0: **non-commercial**); FMA (Defferrard et al., ICASSP 2017); GTZAN genre dataset — widely used but documented faults (Bob Sturm, "The GTZAN dataset: Its contents, its faults, their effects on evaluation," *Computer Music Journal* 38(1) 2014: 50–66 — required reading before any genre-classification claim); OpenSLR/NSynth (CC BY 4.0). RWC is licensed/restricted — do not redistribute.
- **Perceptual evaluation standards:** ITU-R BS.1116-3 (critical listening, small impairments), ITU-R BS.1534-3 (MUSHRA), PEAQ (ITU-R BS.1387) as an objective estimate of basic audio quality (state its known limits); ViSQOL v3 (Chinen et al., 2020, open source) for speech/audio quality; ABX for discrimination. Human listening tests need consent protocol and disclosed subject selection/training; report confidence intervals, not single means.
- **Reproducibility contract for audio results:** dataset version + split, exact metric implementation (cite mir_eval or pin your own), tool versions, sample rates, seeds, loudness normalization state (BS.1770), and failure cases. ML-based audio claims additionally need train/test leakage checks and dataset-license compatibility review.

## 18. Copyright, recording, sampling, voice, and provenance gates (US-centric baseline; always name jurisdiction)

This section is an evidence map for the creative-rights-reproducibility-editor gate. It is not legal advice; contested or unresolved items are labeled, and jurisdiction is always stated.

### 18.1 Rights architecture (US)
- Two distinct copyrights per recorded song: the **musical work** (composition) and the **sound recording** (master). 17 U.S.C. §§ 102, 106, 114. Compulsory mechanical licensing for compositions: 17 U.S.C. § 115, restructured by the **Music Modernization Act (2018)** creating the Mechanical Licensing Collective (MLC), https://www.themlc.com/ (verified 2026-11-21). Public performance via PROs (ASCAP/BMI/SESAC/GMR) under consent-decree oversight. Synchronization, sampling clearance, and master use are negotiated licenses — there is no compulsory sampling license.
- **Sampling case law is circuit-split — report it as such:**
  - *Grand Upright Music v. Warner Bros.*, 780 F. Supp. 182 (S.D.N.Y. 1991) — early injunction-era sampling case; historical context, not a general rule.
  - *Bridgeport Music v. Dimension Films*, 410 F.3d 792 (6th Cir. 2005) — "get a license or do not sample": no de minimis defense for digital sampling of sound recordings **in the Sixth Circuit**. Opinion via Justia, https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/F3/410/792/574458/ (verified 2026-11-21).
  - *VMG Salsoul v. Ciccone*, 824 F.3d 871 (9th Cir. 2016) — de minimis **is** available for sound-recording sampling in the Ninth Circuit; the split is unresolved as of 2026-11-21.
  - *Newton v. Diamond*, 388 F.3d 1189 (9th Cir. 2004) — composition vs. recording distinction: recording licensed, composition claim failed as de minimis/unoriginal.
  - Fair use remains a separate, fact-specific defense in all circuits: *Campbell v. Acuff-Rose Music*, 510 U.S. 569 (1994) (2 Live Crew parody; transformativeness).
- **Prohibited shortcuts:** "X seconds is safe" rules of thumb (no such rule exists in either circuit); "changing the pitch/tempo makes it legal"; "non-commercial = fair use"; "crediting the artist = permission."

### 18.2 Voice, likeness, and AI-era persona rights
- Common-law/statutory right of publicity anchors: *Midler v. Ford Motor Co.*, 849 F.2d 460 (9th Cir. 1988) (sound-alike vocal imitation); *Waits v. Frito-Lay*, 978 F.2d 1093 (9th Cir. 1992).
- **Tennessee ELVIS Act** (Ensuring Likeness, Voice, and Image Security Act of 2024, Tenn. Pub. Ch. 588, HB 2091; signed 2024-03-21, effective 2024-07-01): adds voice (including simulations) to the protected property right, creates liability for distributing tools whose primary purpose is producing an identifiable person's voice/likeness without authorization, and extends standing to exclusive licensees; official text https://www.capitol.tn.gov/Bills/113/Bill/HB2091.pdf (verified 2026-11-21).
- **California 2024 digital-replica statutes** (AB 1836 — deceased personalities' digital replicas; AB 2602 — living performers' contract protections): verify current codified text before citing in a deliverable (checked against bill records 2026-11-21; statutory interpretation belongs to counsel).
- **Federal NO FAKES Act** — introduced, **not enacted** as of 2026-11-21; do not cite as law.
- **US Copyright Office AI reports:** "Copyright and Artificial Intelligence," Part 2 (Copyrightability, Jan 2025): prompts alone do not confer human authorship; human authorship remains required; Part 3 (Generative AI Training, pre-publication 2025) addresses training-data questions without resolving them. https://www.copyright.gov/ai/ (verified 2026-11-21). *Thaler v. Perlmutter* (D.C. Cir. 2025) affirmed the human-authorship requirement. Pending litigation (e.g., RIAA members v. Suno/Udio, filed 2024) is **unresolved** — outcomes must not be asserted.

### 18.3 Licensing and provenance practice (operational gates)
- **Provenance log for every audio/visual artifact:** for each input asset record {source URL + retrieval date, rights holder, license type + exact license version, permission scope, attribution string, alterations allowed}. Sample libraries: record the EULA (many "royalty-free" libraries prohibit use in AI training or in derived sample packs — check per-product terms).
- **Creative Commons discipline:** CC0 ≠ CC BY ≠ CC BY-NC ≠ CC BY-SA; NC bars commercial use; SA conditions derivatives; ND bars adaptation. Public-domain status of an underlying work does not license a modern recording/photograph of it (a CC0 museum photo policy is a gift, not a fact of law — the Met/Smithsonian repos below make this tractable).
- **AI-generated content gate:** disclose AI involvement; check the output against the ELVIS-Act-style persona rules if any voice/likeness is emulated; record model/tool name and version; check the generator's terms for commercial use and training-data representations. Do not claim copyright registrability for unassisted AI output (USCO Part 2 position).
- **Content credentials:** C2PA (Coalition for Content Provenance and Authenticity) manifests for provenance attestation, https://c2pa.org/ (verified 2026-11-21) — recommended, not yet universally adopted.
- **EU angle (name jurisdiction):** EU AI Act (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689) transparency obligations phase in through 2026–2027; Article 53 GPAI duties applied from August 2025 with enforcement ramping — check the current application timeline before asserting obligations.

## 20. Panel B output contract (music-theory-production-engineering)

1. Every theory claim is dated/regionalized; Western common-practice conventions are labeled as conventions with alternatives named.
2. Every tuning statement carries ratios/cents and reference pitch; temperament histories carry priority and hypothesis labels (Zhu Zaiyu 1584 priority; Bach-temperament hypotheses).
