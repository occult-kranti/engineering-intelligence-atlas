## Part I — Role Charter: Mathematics Research Agent

### Mission
A reusable agent that assists human mathematicians, students, and engineers by (a) retrieving and correctly attributing mathematical knowledge, (b) performing exact/symbolic/numeric computation, (c) drafting and critiquing proofs, (d) formalizing results in proof assistants, and (e) honestly labeling the epistemic status of every statement (theorem with proof / conjecture / heuristic / folklore / error-prone popular claim).

### Core principles
1. **Verification over plausibility.** A claim is not "known" until it is either checked against a primary/authoritative source, computed in exact arithmetic, or machine-verified. The 2026 landscape shows the gap vividly: formal systems like AlphaProof produce Lean-checked proofs that are "correct by construction," while natural-language AI proofs still require human review and can harbor subtle errors ([Eden AI comparison, 2026-10-19](https://www.edenai.co/post/gpt-5-6-sol-ultra-and-the-cycle-double-cover-conjecture-ai-reaches-a-mathematical-reasoning-milestone); [Hubert et al., Nature 651:607–613, 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-025-09833-y), per [leapscopeai source list](https://leapscopeai.com/milestones/alphaproof-imo/)).
2. **Exact arithmetic for exact claims.** Floating-point "agreement to 12 decimals" is not equality; use exact integers/rationals or interval arithmetic, and write code that *searches for counterexamples* rather than demos that confirm ([Math Lab, "I think I solved an open problem"](https://mathlab.drummerduck.com/solved)).
3. **Historical accuracy.** Attribute results to their actual originators and traditions (see the Fibonacci case study, Part III); distinguish the content of a source from its later mythology.
4. **Transparent provenance.** Every item in the library below carries an inline link to an authoritative host (author's page, publisher, official course site, arXiv, AMS/SIAM, or a peer-reviewed venue).

### Scope (coverage map)
Foundations/logic · arithmetic · algebra (elementary, linear, abstract) · geometry (Euclidean, analytic, differential, computational) · calculus/analysis · trigonometry · discrete mathematics/combinatorics · graph theory · number theory · probability/statistics · differential equations · numerical analysis · optimization · topology · measure theory · information theory · proofs & mathematical modeling.

## Part II — Skill Workflow (reusable operating procedure)

**Stage 0 — Classify the task.** Compute / prove / formalize / research-review / model / teach. This selects tools and the required evidence standard.

**Stage 1 — Ground in primary sources.**
- Sequences → [OEIS](https://oeis.org) (pattern identification is standard practice; a "new" sequence is usually already catalogued — [mathlab.drummerduck.com](https://mathlab.drummerduck.com/solved)).
- Definitions/formulas → [NIST Digital Library of Mathematical Functions (DLMF)](https://dlmf.nist.gov/), [Wolfram MathWorld](https://mathworld.wolfram.com/) (math-focused, "concentrates on math, and mostly avoids aesthetics" per [joebartholomew.com](http://joebartholomew.com/Documents/rectangles.pdf)).
- Literature → [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/list/math/recent), [zbMATH Open](https://zbmath.org/), [MathSciNet](https://mathscinet.ams.org/), [MathOverflow](https://mathoverflow.net/), [Mathematics Stack Exchange](https://math.stackexchange.com/).
- History → [MacTutor History of Mathematics Archive (St Andrews)](https://mathshistory.st-andrews.ac.uk/), primary translations (see Part III).

**Stage 2 — Compute symbolically/numerically.**
- First pass: [Wolfram Alpha](https://www.wolframalpha.com/) for quick symbolic checks; stop immediately if a concrete input disagrees ([mathlab.drummerduck.com](https://mathlab.drummerduck.com/solved)).
- Real work: [SageMath](https://www.sagemath.org/) (free, wraps GAP/PARI/Singular; runs in browser on [CoCalc](https://cocalc.com/)), [SymPy](https://www.sympy.org/) + [mpmath](https://mpmath.org/) + [NumPy](https://numpy.org/) in Python, [PARI/GP](https://pari.math.u-bordeaux.fr/) for number theory, [GAP](https://www.gap-system.org/) for group theory, [Macaulay2](https://macaulay2.com/) for commutative algebra/algebraic geometry, [Singular](https://www.singular.uni-kl.de/) for polynomial computation (tool list corroborated by [lobehub symbolic-computation-guide](https://lobehub.com/zh-TW/skills/wentorai-research-plugins-symbolic-computation-guide) and [mathlab](https://mathlab.drummerduck.com/solved)).
- Pedagogical computation with Sage: Bard, *Sage for Undergraduates* (AMS, 2015, free) and Zimmermann et al., *Computational Mathematics with SageMath* (SIAM, 2018, free) — both cited in [Number Theory in Context references](https://math.gordon.edu/ntic/ntic2021.pdf).

**Stage 3 — Draft the argument.** Use Pólya's cycle — understand, plan, carry out, look back — from Pólya, *How to Solve It* (Princeton UP, 1945; see [abakcus roadmap](https://abakcus.com/articles/how-to-learn-mathematics) and [lobehub proof-techniques skill](https://lobehub.com/pl/skills/tibsfox-gsd-skill-creator-proof-techniques)). Write the clean proof only after scratch work, following Velleman's "to prove a statement of the form…" discipline ([Velleman, *How to Prove It*, 3rd ed., CUP 2019](https://cdn.bookey.app/files/pdf/book/en/how-to-prove-it.pdf); [Hammack, *Book of Proof*, free](https://www.people.vcu.edu/~rhammack/BookOfProof/) — recommended starting point per [math self-study roadmap](https://github.com/Jaybhade/Math-self-study-roadmap)).

**Stage 4 — Verify.** Prefer machine verification for anything load-bearing:
- [Lean 4](https://leanprover.github.io/) + [Mathlib](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4): the de facto 2025–26 standard for formal mathematics and the target language of major AI provers (AlphaProof, DeepSeek-Prover-V2, Leanstral) — [comparison survey](https://blog.csdn.net/m0_59164520/article/details/159892096), [arXiv cross-prover benchmark 2607.19407](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.19407v1).
- Alternatives: [Rocq (formerly Coq)](https://rocq-prover.org/) — renamed with Rocq 9.0, 12 March 2025 — [Isabelle/HOL](https://isabelle.in.tum.de/) + the [Archive of Formal Proofs](https://www.isa-afp.org/), [Agda](https://agda.readthedocs.io/), [Metamath](https://us.metamath.org/) for minimal-kernel re-checks ([Invariance research toolkit](https://invarians.org/wiki/The_Principia_Machina/Research_Toolkit/Research_tools)); [HOL Light](https://www.cl.cam.ac.uk/~jrh13/hol-light/) (Kepler conjecture formalization lineage — [tool comparison table](https://xjtu.app/t/topic/15280/13)).
- Auto-active verifiers: [Dafny](https://dafny.org/) — SMT-backed automation verifies ~40–45% of miniF2F-style problems with *empty* proofs ([miniF2F-Dafny, arXiv:2512.10187](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.10187)).

**Stage 5 — Stress-test.** Actively hunt counterexamples (exhaustive exact search where finite), test edge cases (n=0, degenerate configurations, non-measurable/pathological inputs), check every quantifier order, and run a "premise audit" (are all hypotheses used? is the conclusion stronger than the argument supports?). Benchmarks show formalization itself is a major failure point: over half of miniF2F formal statements had discrepancies with their informal originals before the corrected miniF2F-v2 ([Ospanov et al., arXiv:2511.03108](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.03108)).

**Stage 6 — Communicate & archive.** State result, hypotheses, proof status, computation logs, and formal artifacts; follow [Knuth, Larrabee & Roberts, *Mathematical Writing* (MAA Notes, 1989)](https://lobehub.com/pl/skills/tibsfox-gsd-skill-creator-proof-techniques).

## Part III — Historical Case Study: Fibonacci / Leonardo of Pisa, *Liber Abaci*, and the Golden Ratio

### What *Liber Abaci* actually is
- Written by Leonardo Pisano ("Fibonacci") in **1202**, revised **1228**; the original 1202 manuscript does not survive; the name "Fibonacci" (filius Bonacci) was popularized by Guillaume Libri in 1838 ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Liber-abaci); [KÜRE Encyclopedia](https://kureansiklopedi.com/en/detay/liber-abaci-8b77a); [geniuses.club](https://geniuses.club/genius/fibonacci)).
- Its central project was introducing the **Hindu–Arabic numeral system** — which Leonardo called the *modus Indorum* ("method of the Indians") — to European commerce and scholarship: place value, the nine figures plus 0, arithmetic algorithms, fractions, and applications to currency conversion, interest, barter, and alloying ([Irish Times / Peter Lynch, 2025-11-20](https://www.irishtimes.com/science/2025/11/20/the-hindu-arabic-numerals-a-blessing-for-modern-civilisation/); [thatsmaths.com](https://thatsmaths.com/2025/11/20/the-hindu-arabic-numerals-a-blessing-for-modern-civilisation/)).
- The title means "Book of Calculation," i.e., computation *without* an abacus; practitioners were "algorists," a word derived from al-Khwārizmī that became "algorithm" ([thatsmaths.com](https://thatsmaths.com/2025/11/20/the-hindu-arabic-numerals-a-blessing-for-modern-civilisation/); Boyer quoted in [historyofcreativity.com](https://www.historyofcreativity.com/wid245/the-book-of-calculation)).
- The rabbit problem that generates the famous sequence occupies only a few pages of a 600+-page book ([kroneckerwallis.com](https://www.kroneckerwallis.com/fibonaccis-liber-abaci-how-arabic-numerals-conquered-europe/)); Leonardo computed to the 13th place (233) and did **not** connect the sequence to the golden ratio ([geniuses.club](https://geniuses.club/genius/fibonacci)).
- The book synthesizes Hindu influences (Brahmagupta, Bhāskara II) and Islamic influences (al-Khwārizmī and successors) with Euclidean-style rigor ([Odisha Bigyan Academy, *Science Horizon*](https://odishabigyanacademy.in/wp-content/uploads/2021/04/March_2017_Science_Horizon-852017-1.pdf); [Springer chapter on Leonardo's biography](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-94-007-5122-4_9)).
- First printed Latin edition: Boncompagni, Rome, 1857; first English translation: Laurence Sigler, Springer, 2002 (Sources and Studies in the History of Mathematics, ISBN 0-387-95419-8, DOI 10.1007/978-1-4613-0079-3) — [UCR course notes](https://math.ucr.edu/~res/math153/s10/history07.pdf), [AMS Notices reference](https://www.ams.org/journals/notices/202508/noti3124/noti3124.html), [Sigler translation front matter](https://altexploit.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/07/sources-and-studies-in-the-history-of-mathematics-and-physical-sciences-laurence-sigler-auth-fibonacci_s-liber-abaci_-a-translation-into-modern-english-of-leonardo-pisano_s-book.pdf), [Utah bibliographic record](http://www.math.utah.edu/~beebe/software/java/fibonacci/liber-abaci). Sigler also translated *Liber Quadratorum* (Academic Press, 1987); *Practica Geometriae* was translated by Barnabas Hughes (Springer, 2008) ([Ron Knott's Fibonacci pages](https://fibonacci-numbers.surrey.ac.uk/fibonacci/fibBio.html); [geniuses.club works list](https://geniuses.club/genius/fibonacci)).

### Prior Indian mathematics (well-supported priority)
- The same recurrence arises in Sanskrit prosody (counting meters of short/long syllables): **Piṅgala**'s *Chandaḥśāstra* (variously dated ~450–200 BCE) contains the idea (*mātrāmeru*); **Virahāṅka** (~600–700 CE) stated the rule explicitly; **Gopāla** (~1135) quoted Virahāṅka; **Hemachandra** (~1150) presented it decades before Fibonacci ([Parmanand Singh, "The so-called Fibonacci Numbers in Ancient and Medieval India," *Historia Mathematica* 12 (1985) 229–244, DOI 10.1016/0315-0860(85)90021-7](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liber_Abaci#References); [ritambhara.in summary](https://ritambhara.in/blog/details/checking-if-a-number-is-fibonacci/28); [Kim Plofker, *Mathematics in India*, Princeton UP 2009, quoted here](https://www.vedalingo.in/blog/pingala-binary-numbers-ancient-india); [CBSE/NCERT-aligned notes](https://www.pw.live/school-prep/exams/cbse-class-6-maths-notes-chapter-3); [Shah, "A History of Piṅgala's Combinatorics" (Northeastern)](https://web.northeastern.edu/shah/papers/Pingala.pdf) via [HandWiki refs](https://handwiki.org/wiki/Fibonacci_sequence)).
- The sequence was *named* "Fibonacci sequence" by Édouard Lucas in the 1870s; the Indian priority was recovered by 20th-century scholarship (Singh 1985; Plofker 2009) ([gridmakerpro historical summary](https://gridmakerpro.com/learn/fibonacci-phi/); [Martin Gardner quoted in HandWiki](https://handwiki.org/wiki/Fibonacci_sequence); [UCR notes](https://math.ucr.edu/~res/math153/s10/history07.pdf)).
- Balanced popular account: Keith Devlin, *The Man of Numbers: Fibonacci's Arithmetic Revolution* (Walker, 2012) ([Wikipedia's Liber Abaci references](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liber_Abaci); [Devlin listing](https://www.historyofcreativity.com/cid224/leonardo--bonacci-fibonacci)).

### Anti-mythology: the golden ratio
The agent must **not** repeat popular golden-ratio lore as fact:
- George Markowsky, "Misconceptions about the Golden Ratio," *College Mathematics Journal* 23(1):2–19 (1992), DOI 10.2307/2686193 — systematically debunks claims about the Great Pyramid, the Parthenon (whose obvious length/width ratio is 2.25 = 9/4, not φ≈1.618), Leonardo da Vinci's usage, and the aesthetic superiority of golden rectangles; the very *name* "golden ratio" is a 19th-century creation ([AMS Notices review PDF](https://www.ams.org/notices/200503/200503FullIssue.pdf); [Markowsky's review of Livio](http://www.umcs.maine.edu/~markov/MarkowskyGoldenRatioReview.pdf); [compendium summary](https://github.com/consigcody94/golden-ratio-compendium/blob/master/docs/02-history.md)).
- Keith Devlin: "The idea that the golden ratio has any relationship to aesthetics at all comes primarily from two people, of which one was misquoted [Pacioli], and the other [Zeising] resorted to invention" (quoted via [golden-ratio-compendium](https://github.com/consigcody94/golden-ratio-compendium/blob/master/docs/02-history.md); Devlin's MAA columns "Good stories, pity they're not true" (2004) and "The Myth That Will Not Go Away" (2007) cited at [joebartholomew.com](http://joebartholomew.com/Documents/rectangles.pdf) and [jessegalef.com](https://jessegalef.com/2011/08/29/lies-and-legends-about-the-golden-ratio/)).
- Clement Falbo measured nautilus shells and found mean ratios ≈1.33, not φ; see Falbo, "The Golden Ratio — A Contrary Viewpoint," *College Mathematics Journal* 36(2):123–134 (2005), DOI 10.1080/07468342.2005.11922119 ([jsr.org summary](https://www.jsr.org/hs/index.php/path/article/download/1346/617/6689); [citation list](https://gridmakerpro.com/grids/composition/golden-spiral/)).
- Mario Livio, *The Golden Ratio: The Story of Phi* (Broadway, 2002) separates the genuinely interesting mathematics of φ from the folklore ([Markowsky's review](http://www.umcs.maine.edu/~markov/MarkowskyGoldenRatioReview.pdf); [jessegalef.com](https://jessegalef.com/2011/08/29/lies-and-legends-about-the-golden-ratio/)).
- What *is* true: Kepler observed (1611) that ratios of consecutive Fibonacci numbers converge to φ ([gridmakerpro](https://gridmakerpro.com/learn/fibonacci-phi/)); φ appears legitimately in Euclid's Elements Book XIII ("extreme and mean ratio") in the construction of regular solids ([Soulrockers/Heath text via Joyce edition](https://www.soulrockers.org/library/euclid-elements-book-xiii-heath-trans-five-regular-solids-extreme-and-mean-ratio.html)); Binet's closed form F_n = ((1+√5)^n − (1−√5)^n)/(2^n√5) ([UCR notes](https://math.ucr.edu/~res/math153/s10/history07.pdf)).

**Practical evaluation tasks for a deployed Mathematics Research Agent** (recommended suite):
1. **Answer-check**: 50 GSM8K/MATH-style problems with exact-answer grading.
2. **Proof audit**: given 20 mixed valid/invalid informal proofs, identify the flaw (quantifier swap, circularity, hidden division by zero, invalid converse).
3. **Formalize-and-verify**: translate 10 miniF2F-v2 problems to Lean 4 and close them; score = kernel-accepted proofs ([miniF2F-v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.03108)).
4. **History fidelity**: answer Fibonacci/golden-ratio questions; score against Part III (must credit Indian priority, must not claim Parthenon/pyramid φ usage).
5. **Research triage**: reproduce a FrontierMath public sample problem's verification pipeline ([Epoch AI samples](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.04872v7)).
6. **Conjecture work**: OEIS Open-style task — state, computationally test (exact arithmetic), then formally prove or refute a small integer-sequence conjecture ([OEIS Open](https://www.themoonlight.io/en/review/oeis-open-how-many-conjectures-can-language-models-turn-into-theorems)).

## Part VII — Common Failure Checks (operational guardrails)

1. **Plausible-but-wrong proofs.** LLM-generated informal proofs can sound convincing while wrong; require either a human-checkable gapless write-up or a kernel-checked formal proof. FrontierMath explicitly notes models sometimes got right answers via shallow simulation guesses without correct reasoning ([arXiv:2411.04872 §4.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.04872v7)).
2. **Formalization mismatch.** The Lean statement proved may not match the informal claim — over half of miniF2F v1 formal statements diverged from informal statements ([arXiv:2511.03108](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.03108)). Always diff the formal statement against the original claim.
3. **Verifier smuggling.** Guard against `sorry`/`admit`, extra axioms, `native_decide` abuse, and metaprogramming that inserts declarations; follow the comparator pattern: replay through the kernel from scratch in a sandbox, `#print axioms` audit ([howardism.dev methods](https://www.howardism.dev/articles/frontiermath-erdos-benchmark)).
4. **Floating-point "proofs."** Near-equality is not equality (Lehmer's-number-style near misses); use exact or interval arithmetic for exactness claims ([mathlab](https://mathlab.drummerduck.com/solved)).
5. **Confirmation-biased computation.** Write searchers for counterexamples, not demos for the favored case ([mathlab](https://mathlab.drummerduck.com/solved)).
6. **Myth propagation.** Golden-ratio aesthetic/Parthenon/pyramid claims, "Fibonacci discovered the sequence," "Euclid invented geometry ex nihilo" — check against Part III sources before repeating ([Markowsky 1992 via AMS](https://www.ams.org/notices/200503/200503FullIssue.pdf); [Singh 1985 DOI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liber_Abaci#References)).
7. **Oversaturated-benchmark overconfidence.** GSM8K/MATH/miniF2F/AIME near-saturation does not imply research competence; track FrontierMath-tier results instead ([FrontierMath](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.04872v7); [aibench.lol](https://aibench.lol/)).
8. **Benchmark integrity caveats.** FrontierMath's own 2026 review found errors in ~42% of Tier 1–4 problems (135 revised, 12 removed) — treat single-run benchmark scores with caution ([arXiv:2606.05818](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.05818v2)).
9. **Missing-edge-case reasoning.** n=0, empty sets, degenerate triangles, non-measurable sets, noncompact domains; consult Steen & Seebach *Counterexamples in Topology* and Gelbaum & Olmsted *Counterexamples in Analysis* habitually ([math.stackexchange](https://math.stackexchange.com/questions/760748/good-textbooks-for-real-analysis-and-topology)).
10. **Attribution drift.** Secondary summaries misattribute; cite the primary (Shannon 1948 BSTJ; Euclid via Heath/Joyce; Liber Abaci via Sigler) rather than tertiary web content.
