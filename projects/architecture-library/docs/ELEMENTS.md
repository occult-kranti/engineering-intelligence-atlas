# Elements and structural reading

The element collection contains **30 concise teaching records**. Each record identifies a form or assembly, gives a qualitative load-path explanation, lists illustrative materials and uses, and records limits and source IDs. The authoritative machine-readable files are [elements.json](../data/elements.json) and [sources-elements.json](../data/sources-elements.json). The sample index is [samples.json](../data/samples.json).

The definitions are original paraphrases. A load-path paragraph is an educational explanation of a general principle, **not an analysis of a named monument or of the supplied mesh**. No load cases, member capacities, foundations, construction sequencing, fire strategy, accessibility assessment, conservation approval or applicable code checks have been completed.

## Read related elements together

| Group | Element IDs | What to compare |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Uprights and spanning members | `standing-stone`, `post-and-lintel`, `column`, `capital`, `entablature` | An upright's support role differs from the bending and bearing in a horizontal span. The classical order also organizes relationships and meaning beyond a generic structural diagram. |
| Monumental massing | `stepped-pyramid`, `smooth-pyramid`, `terrace-platform`, `monumental-stair` | Similar outlines do not establish shared origins or functions. Separate Egyptian funerary settings from the Maya examples informing the terrace study. |
| Curved masonry | `true-arch`, `voussoir`, `barrel-vault`, `pier`, `dome`, `oculus` | An arch or vault requires a complete supporting system; an opening in a dome changes the surface through which forces pass. |
| Gothic assemblies | `pointed-arch`, `rib-vault`, `flying-buttress` | Follow a bay into its supports and surrounding fabric. The sample leaves out vault webs to expose its ribs. |
| Southern Indian temple context | `vimana`, `corbel` | The supplied tower is a Dravida-inspired sanctum-tower study scoped to Tamil Chola references. It is not a gopuram or a general model of Indian temples. |
| Timber roof assemblies | `timber-post-beam`, `bracket-set`, `purlin` | Distinguish the main frame, roof members and bracket transition. The Chinese bay's near roof covering is removed to show the assembly. |
| Spatial organization | `courtyard`, `arcade` | A courtyard describes an arrangement of space. An arcade describes a repeated architectural assembly; neither alone specifies a complete building. |
| Modern networks and surfaces | `diagrid`, `gridshell`, `space-frame`, `truss`, `shell` | A diagonal facade pattern, curved lattice or triangulated mesh needs engineered joints, boundaries and stabilization before it can be treated as a working structure. |

## Evidence and cultural scope

English Heritage's [Stonehenge stone descriptions](https://www.english-heritage.org.uk/visit/places/stonehenge/things-to-do/stone-circle/stones-of-stonehenge) inform the upright/lintel relationship; the sample's twelve-post ring and dimensions are invented. The Smithsonian's [pyramid overview](https://www.si.edu/spotlight/ancient-egypt/pyramid) distinguishes stepped and smooth forms. INAH's [Chichen Itza descriptions](https://lugares.inah.gob.mx/es/node/4335) provide a separate Maya context for raised platforms and stairs. No astronomical alignment or inter-cultural transmission is inferred from the models.

The Met's [Greek architecture essay](https://www.metmuseum.org/essays/architecture-in-ancient-greece) supports the vocabulary of column, capital and entablature. The [V&A introduction](https://www.vam.ac.uk/articles/the-gothic-style-an-introduction) supports the Gothic grouping. UNESCO's [Great Living Chola Temples record](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/250/) anchors the vimana terminology in a specified regional and historical setting. [Prince Kung's Palace Museum](https://en.pgm.org.cn/2024-04/18/c_1122470.htm) describes bracket sets as a transition in Chinese timber architecture. A single schematic bay does not stand for every Chinese period, regional practice or East Asian timber tradition.

The courtyard study uses the [Alhambra custodian's account](https://www.alhambra-patronato.es/en/edificios-lugares/the-court-of-the-lions) as a spatial reference without reproducing its geometry or ornament. Modern entries use firsthand engineering or system descriptions: [WSP on Hearst Tower](https://www.wsp.com/en-us/projects/hearst-tower-new-york), the [Savill gridshell paper's public abstract](https://www.istructe.org/journal/volumes/volume-86-(published-in-2008)/issue-17/the-savill-garden-gridshell-design-and-constructio/), and [MERO's space-frame explanation](https://www.mero.com.sg/fa%C3%A7ade-system/spaceframe-system/). Project claims are not transferred into performance claims for these samples.

The source register uses the review date **2026-11-24, America/New_York**. Review depth was the relevant public page text or search-indexed source excerpt, not whole books, paid articles, survey archives or full conservation dossiers. In particular, only the public abstract of the Savill paper was read. Direct fetches of the Smithsonian page, the Italian Ministry's Pantheon page, Prince Kung's bracket page and some Getty records were blocked or timed out in this session; the relevant indexed institutional excerpts were available. These access limits do not establish permission to redistribute the underlying publications.

## Geometry is deliberately limited

All 14 samples were authored procedurally for this collection. Dimensions are **metres in Blender's right-handed Z-up scene**; GLB export uses glTF's Y-up convention. The display plinth is included in each metadata bounding box, object count and triangle count. Colour distinguishes parts and does not claim historical material finish or original polychromy.

The sample files contain no scans, downloaded models, external textures, maps or copyrighted site imagery. Repository terms apply to these original files.
