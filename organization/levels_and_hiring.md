## Responsibility levels

Use SFIA-style responsibility dimensions rather than copying company-specific titles. The following is a local reference model, not a mapping of Amazon, Meta, Google, Netflix, or Rockstar levels.

| Local level | Autonomy and complexity | Influence and scope | Evidence expected |
|---|---|---|---|
| L1 Follow/trainee | Performs structured tasks under close guidance | Learns team practices | Foundational exercises, learning agility, supervised delivery |
| L2 Assist/associate | Handles defined tasks and routine problems | Contributes within a small work area | Working features/tests/docs with review; explains choices |
| L3 Apply/independent | Handles varied non-routine work with limited direction | Owns a component or service slice | End-to-end delivery, debugging, operations, collaboration |
| L4 Enable/senior | Resolves ambiguous cross-component problems | Guides others and improves team capability | Technical leadership, design tradeoffs, mentoring, incident learning |
| L5 Ensure/advise/staff | Sets direction for multiple teams or a difficult subsystem | Influences without line authority | Cross-team outcomes, strategy, reusable mechanisms, risk reduction |
| L6 Initiate/influence/principal | Shapes organizational technical or functional strategy | Company/domain-wide influence | Multi-org technical bets, standards, succession and capability building |
| L7 Set strategy/distinguished/executive | Sets long-term strategy under broad accountability | Organization or industry-scale influence | Sustained strategic outcomes and stewardship |

SFIA 9 reference: https://sfia-online.org/en/sfia-9. Public ladder examples: https://dropbox.github.io/dbx-career-framework/ and https://handbook.gitlab.com/handbook/engineering/careers/

## Dual ladder

Keep senior IC and management paths distinct and similarly valued. Management requires people stewardship, capacity planning, hiring, performance management, organizational design, and delivery systems; it is not a promotion prize for technical excellence. Staff-plus IC roles require technical direction, influence, judgment, and leverage; they are not managers without direct reports unless explicitly scoped. Track switches should include a reversible acting period and explicit support.

## Role scorecard template

1. **Mission:** one sentence describing the value-stream outcome.
2. **Accountable outputs:** 3–6 observable artifacts or operational outcomes.
3. **Must-have capabilities:** only capabilities needed in the first 6–12 months.
4. **Preferred exposure:** genuinely helpful but learnable after hiring.
5. **Responsibility level:** autonomy, complexity, influence, scope, and knowledge.
6. **Interfaces:** PM/design/engineering/data/security/SRE/QA/docs/production partners.
7. **Evidence rubric:** work sample, debugging or critique, design/research judgment, operational behavior, communication, ethics/safety.
8. **Non-requirements:** unnecessary degree, years, pedigree, framework, or keyword filters removed.

Use separate anchors for 1=below, 2=partial, 3=meets, 4=exceeds. Require evidence for each rating. Do not average away a critical safety, integrity, or foundational-skill failure.

## Interview controls

Use structured questions and the same core rubric for candidates for the same role. Train interviewers, separate assessment from team enthusiasm, document evidence, and review adverse impact and accessibility of the process. Use independent bar review or hiring committees where feasible, but do not claim practitioner-described Big Tech processes are official or immutable. Give candidates the required accommodations and clear logistics. Never use an AI score as the sole hiring decision; validate job relevance, bias, privacy, and human review under applicable law.

For research roles, distinguish scientific novelty, experimental rigor, publication/reproduction evidence, engineering execution, and product transition. For design/game roles, evaluate process and playable/prototyped outcomes, not only visual polish. For security and reverse engineering, include authorization, evidence preservation, limitations, and defensive handoff—not only technical exploitation ability.
